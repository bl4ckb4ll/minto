# Reader first

A first draft can be self-focused. It can be a dump of thoughts, feelings, observations, and half-formed requests.

Revision has a different job: form an accurate model of the person receiving the message.

Before sending, ask:

- Why am I telling this person this?
- What do they already know?
- What do they not know that matters here?
- What do they not need to know?
- What authority do they have?
- What decision can they make?
- What action can they take?
- What, exactly, am I asking them to do?
- What should they hear first?
- How much of their time does this deserve?

The goal is not terseness for its own sake. The goal is to convert private thought into communication designed for another person.

This applies to business communication, but it is not primarily about corporate writing. It applies just as much to a text message, a request to a friend, instructions, a bug report, a note to a teacher, a family message, or a technical explanation.

A useful drafting sequence is:

1. Write what you are actually trying to say.
2. Identify the recipient.
3. Identify the recipient's relevant knowledge, authority, constraints, and likely question.
4. Put the thing they need first.
5. Remove material that serves only the writer's need to unload.
6. Keep context that the reader genuinely needs.
7. Make the requested action, if any, explicit.

The ethical part matters: another person's attention is not an infinite sink for whatever happens to be in the writer's head. Writing well means recognizing that the recipient is also a person.
