# Repair types

Mathematical writing can fail locally or globally. The corpus should label which level is being repaired.

## Local repairs

- replace an opaque sentence with a direct one;
- unpack an overloaded pronoun or reference;
- move a definition closer to first use;
- rename notation to expose a pattern;
- split a sentence whose logical dependencies are hidden;
- merge choppy sentences when the argument is really one thought;
- replace an unexplained symbol with a brief semantic cue.

## Paragraph and proof repairs

- state the proof strategy before technical execution;
- separate the invariant from the bookkeeping;
- move a motivating example before the abstraction it motivates;
- surface the reason for a construction;
- remove a detour that is not used;
- delay a technical lemma until the reader can see why it is needed.

## Section and chapter repairs

- reorder dependencies;
- move examples beside the concepts they illuminate;
- extract a clean through-line from a reference-style chapter;
- split one chapter into conceptual and technical paths;
- provide multiple reading paths with explicit prerequisites.

## Book-scale repairs

### Slice
Keep only material needed for one goal.

Example:
"Commutative algebra needed to understand this one geometric construction", rather than a miniature copy of the original textbook.

### Reorder
Preserve nearly all material but change dependency order.

### Sub-book
Construct a coherent path through pieces of a larger work. Record:
- original source order;
- new order;
- omitted material;
- added bridges;
- dependency assumptions.

### Style-preserving rewrite
Clarify while trying to keep the author's mathematical voice.

### Style-changing rewrite
Preserve the mathematics while deliberately changing the expository mode: terse to discursive, formal to geometric, theorem-first to example-first, etc.

## Evaluation

A repair is not automatically an improvement. Check:
- mathematical correctness;
- dependency correctness;
- whether motivation became clearer;
- whether the revised version is easier to resume after interruption;
- whether important nuance was removed;
- whether the new version falsely suggests a simpler logical structure than the mathematics actually has.
