# Reference writers

This is a working reference set, not a ranking and not a canon.

The important unit is usually a passage, chapter, proof, diagram sequence, or structural choice—not the author's reputation as a whole.

## Strong positive references

### Allen Hatcher
Useful for:
- geometric motivation before abstraction;
- diagrams that carry mathematical work rather than decorate it;
- making a long argument feel traversable;
- informal explanation without abandoning precision.

Candidate source:
- *Algebraic Topology*.

### John Conway
Useful for:
- compression without deadness;
- strong mathematical voice;
- examples that expose structure;
- memorable notation and problem framing.

Candidate sources:
- expository books and problem-oriented writing.

### John Baez
Useful for:
- explanation through repeated reformulation;
- connecting formal statements to intuitive pictures;
- making sophisticated material readable without pretending it is elementary.

### Miles Reid
Useful for:
- algebraic geometry written with a strong sense of objects and examples;
- concrete geometric language;
- giving the reader a route into intimidating material.

### David Eisenbud and Joe Harris
Useful for:
- balancing geometry, algebra, examples, and historical/intuitive framing;
- chapter-scale organization;
- giving a subject more than one conceptual entrance.

Candidate sources:
- *3264 and All That*;
- related algebraic-geometry texts.

### Frank Adams
Useful for:
- severe technical writing that still makes the logical architecture visible;
- pacing definitions and lemmas so that proofs remain navigable.

### Justin Curry
Useful for:
- category/sheaf/topology exposition with modern notation while retaining motivation;
- explaining why constructions are being introduced.

### Jeremy Gray
Useful for:
- historically informed mathematical exposition by someone who understands the mathematics itself;
- reconstructing older mathematical viewpoints without reducing them to anecdotes;
- making conceptual change visible across time.

Candidate sources:
- work on geometry, analysis, Poincaré, and the history of modern mathematics.

### Mathematical historians with substantial mathematical command
This is a category worth collecting separately. The desired feature is historical writing in which the mathematical content remains live rather than being replaced by broad cultural summary.

Candidates to investigate:
- Jeremy Gray;
- John Stillwell;
- Ivor Grattan-Guinness;
- Leo Corry;
- Reviel Netz;
- other historians/translators working directly with primary mathematical texts.

### Newton translation / reconstruction reference
Two nearby references should be kept distinct:
- I. Bernard Cohen and Anne Whitman — the 1999 modern English translation of Newton's *Principia*.
- S. Chandrasekhar — physicist and author of *Newton's Principia for the Common Reader* (1995), a mathematical analysis/reconstruction rather than the translation itself.

The recalled “physicist who did a translation of Newton” may conflate these two; preserve the distinction until the intended reference is confirmed.

## Mixed / diagnostic references

### Terence Tao
Not a simple negative example.

Useful for:
- decomposing difficult ideas into manageable pieces;
- identifying the real technical obstruction;
- providing multiple routes to an argument;
- adapting exposition to the specific mathematical problem.

Possible negative-study axis:
- prose style can become over-segmented, heavily signposted, or mechanically sequential;
- local clarity does not always add up to strong chapter-level rhythm or memorable architecture.

Use paired examples: preserve the mathematical decomposition while testing whether the prose can be made less procedural and more structurally legible.

## Corpus rule

For each writer, try to collect:
- one passage that works unusually well;
- one passage that can be improved;
- one structural example at chapter/book level;
- one rewrite that preserves mathematical content but changes exposition;
- notes on what would be lost by the rewrite.

The goal is to learn operations, not personalities.
