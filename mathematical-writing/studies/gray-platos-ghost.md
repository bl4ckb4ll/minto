# Jeremy Gray — *Plato's Ghost*

## Source anchor
*Plato's Ghost* organizes the transformation of mathematics roughly from 1880 through the early twentieth century around mathematical modernism, including changes in definitions, proof, language, foundations, and the professional self-conception of mathematicians.

## What to study
This is useful for a different reason from a textbook:
- the mathematics remains an active part of the historical explanation;
- technical changes are connected to changes in what mathematicians thought they were doing;
- chronology is not the only organizing principle.

## Structural experiment
Choose one mathematical idea that changes across the book—for example axiomatics, set-theoretic objects, or the relation between geometry and formalism—and produce:

1. the chronological reading path;
2. an idea-centered "sub-book" that gathers only the relevant episodes;
3. short connective passages explaining the jumps in time.

Measure what the thematic reordering clarifies and what historical causality it risks destroying.

This is an important counterexample to the idea that reordering is free: a cleaner conceptual through-line can falsify history if dependencies in time are erased.
