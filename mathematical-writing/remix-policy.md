# Remix and attribution policy

The purpose of a remix is to make a mathematical path easier to follow, not to obscure authorship.

## Provenance

Every remixed unit should record:
- original author(s);
- original work;
- edition/version when known;
- original chapter/section/page or theorem identifiers when known;
- what was omitted, reordered, paraphrased, or newly written;
- who is responsible for any new errors.

## Credit

Credit the original author for the mathematical exposition and source material that came from them. Do not present a rearrangement or rewrite as if the underlying mathematics or exposition were newly authored here.

A useful default notice:

> This is a reorganized and partly rewritten reading path based on the cited source. The original author receives credit for the source exposition and mathematics. Reordering, omissions, connective text, and any introduced mistakes belong to this remix.

## Copyright

Do not commit full copyrighted books or long copied passages merely because they are useful training material.

Prefer:
- public-domain sources;
- works whose licenses permit redistribution;
- short quoted passages when appropriate;
- structural descriptions;
- original paraphrases;
- pointers to lawful copies.

For copyrighted works, a "sub-book" should normally be a map, commentary, set of references, and original connective prose unless redistribution rights are clear.

## Thanking or supporting authors

Where practical, source notes may include legitimate ways to support or thank the author:
- publisher or author page for buying the book;
- library information;
- author-maintained free edition;
- institutional page;
- other clearly author-approved support route.

This should reduce friction for a reader who finds the remixed path valuable and wants to find or support the original work.
